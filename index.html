    Experience

    Research Assistant in Finance

    ZHAW School of Management and Law
    2024–2026

    As a Research Assistant at ZHAW, I worked on an SNSF-funded research project investigating the unintended consequences of post-crisis banking regulation for firms, banks, and credit markets. My work focused on the corporate finance part of the project, which studies whether regulatory capital requirements affect firms' financing choices around a regulatory revenue threshold.

    A central part of my role was to translate a complex regulatory setting into an empirical research design. In particular, I worked on the Basel III finalization framework and the regulatory treatment of large corporate exposures, where certain senior unsecured exposures to firms above a EUR 500 million revenue threshold are subject to a standardized loss-given-default assumption. This creates a natural setting to study whether firms close to the threshold adjust their reliance on bank debt, market debt, secured debt, short-term borrowing, and overall debt structure.

    • Built and maintained data pipelines for large-scale financial datasets, including Compustat, Capital IQ, and CRSP.
    • Collected, cleaned, merged, and validated firm-level accounting data, debt structure data, and market data across multiple sources.
    • Constructed research variables related to leverage, bank debt, public debt, secured debt, short-term debt, debt maturity, firm size, and regulatory threshold assignment.
    • Designed and implemented screening procedures to ensure that the final sample, descriptive statistics, and constructed variables were economically meaningful and consistent with standards in empirical finance research.
    • Worked on the empirical design for testing financing behavior around the EUR 500 million revenue threshold, including sharp and fuzzy regression discontinuity logic and difference-in-differences extensions.
    • Implemented data quality checks, sample filters, and consistency tests to verify that financing ratios were supported by valid underlying accounting and debt data.
    • Reviewed academic literature in banking regulation, capital requirements, capital structure, debt structure, and empirical identification, and used it to guide variable construction, benchmarking, and robustness checks.
    • Prepared structured documentation of the codebase, data sources, variable construction, screening logic, and workflow to support reproducibility and smooth project handover.
    • Collaborated with academic supervisors and research partners across UZH and ZHAW on empirical strategy, data limitations, and research design.

    Tools and data: Python, pandas, NumPy, Stata, WRDS, Compustat, Capital IQ, CRSP, financial statement data, debt structure data, and market data.

    Methods: panel data analysis, regression discontinuity design, difference-in-differences logic, robustness checks, placebo test design, data cleaning, merging, validation, and reproducible research workflows.

    This role strengthened my ability to work at the intersection of finance, data, and regulation. I learned how to move from an institutional rule to a testable empirical question, how to construct a reliable dataset from imperfect financial data sources, and how to evaluate whether the data are strong enough to support credible empirical analysis.
